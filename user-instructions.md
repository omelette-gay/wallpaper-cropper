# How to use Wallpaper Cropper

Wallpaper Cropper turns your photos into wallpapers that fit your screen exactly. You choose your screen size, pick which part of each photo to keep, and download the results.

**Your photos never leave your device.** Everything happens inside your browser, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

---

## Quick start

1. **Pick your screen size** at the top of the page.
2. **Add your photos** by dragging them onto the page, or click the box to choose them.
3. **Move the frame** on each photo to the part you want, then press **Crop & next**.
4. Press **Finish**, then **Download all (.zip)**.

That's all you need. The sections below explain each step in more detail.

---

## 1. Choose your screen size

Click one of the preset buttons, for example **1440p**, **4K**, **21:9 UW** (ultrawide) or **Phone**.

If your screen isn't listed, type its width and height into the two boxes underneath.

> **Not sure what size your screen is?**
> - **Windows:** right-click the desktop, choose **Display settings**, and look at **Display resolution**.
> - **Mac:** Apple menu, **System Settings**, then **Displays**.
> - **Phone:** search the web for your phone model plus "screen resolution".

## 2. Choose the file type (optional)

The default, **JPEG** at quality **92**, suits nearly everyone. You only need to change this if you have a reason to:

| Format | Good for |
|---|---|
| **JPEG** | Photos. Small files, works everywhere. |
| **PNG** | Perfect quality, but much larger files. |
| **WebP** | Smaller than JPEG at the same quality, but a few older programs can't open it. |

The **Quality** slider makes files sharper (higher) or smaller (lower).

Your choices are remembered for next time.

## 3. Add your photos

Drag photos onto the dashed box, or click it to pick them. You can add as many as you like at once.

JPEG, PNG and WebP photos all work.

> **iPhone users:** iPhone photos are often saved as HEIC, which most browsers can't open. Change them to JPEG first. Choosing **Most Compatible** under **Settings → Camera → Formats** makes your iPhone save new photos as JPEG.

## 4. Position the crop

Each photo opens with a frame on it. Everything inside the frame becomes your wallpaper, and the darkened area is cut away. The frame starts as large as it can be, so you keep as much of the photo as possible.

**To move the frame:** drag it, or click anywhere on the photo to jump it there.

**To zoom in** (a smaller frame, keeping less of the photo): use the **Zoom** slider or scroll your mouse wheel.

**If you lose your place:** press **Re-centre** to put the frame back in the middle.

When you're happy with it:

- **Crop & next** saves this photo and moves on to the next one.
- **Skip** leaves this photo out.
- **Previous** goes back one photo.
- You can also click any thumbnail along the bottom to jump to that photo. A green tick means it's done, and a faded thumbnail means it was skipped.

### "This photo is smaller than…"

If you see this yellow message, the part of the photo you've chosen has fewer pixels than your screen, so it has to be stretched to fit. Stretched photos can look a bit soft or blurry.

When this happens, an **AI enhance upscaling** box appears. With it ticked, the app uses AI to fill in fine detail rather than just stretching the photo, which usually looks much sharper.

Good to know:

- The first time you use it, it downloads a small file (about 4 MB).
- It's slower. A few seconds on a computer, and possibly a minute or more on a phone.
- It all still happens on your device. Nothing is uploaded.

Zooming in makes stretching worse, so zoom out if you want to avoid it.

## 5. Download your wallpapers

When you've been through your photos, press **Finish**. You'll see all your finished wallpapers together.

- **Download all (.zip)** saves them all in a single zip file.
- Or press **Save** under any picture to download just that one.
- **Back to editing** lets you change any you're not happy with.

To start again with new photos, press **Start over** in the top corner. If you've already cropped some photos, you'll be asked to click it a second time, so you don't lose them by accident.

---

## Keyboard shortcuts (computer only)

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| Arrow keys | Move the frame (hold **Shift** to move faster) |
| **+** / **−** or mouse wheel | Zoom in and out |
| **Enter** | Crop & next |
| **S** | Skip |
| **[** / **]** | Previous / next photo |
| **R** | Re-centre |

---

## Tips

- **Light or dark mode:** the sun/moon button in the top corner switches between them.
- **Phones and tablets** work too. Drag the frame with your finger and use the Zoom slider.
- **Got a blurry result?** Choose a bigger part of the photo (zoom out), turn on AI enhance, or use a larger original photo.
